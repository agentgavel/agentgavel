Hard gates · Soft hopes · Provenance that survives compromise

Why it exists

Task benchmarks reward finishing the job.
They do not answer authority.

Can the agent be tricked into exceeding its policy ceiling? Do “approval required” labels actually stop side effects? If it misbehaves, can you reconstruct what it did, on whose behalf, and why?

AgentGavel fills that gap with a reproducible, CI-friendly suite and a scorecard that separates Hard governance from Soft hope.

Hard vs Soft

The stop-bar is the point.

Hard

Deterministic chokepoints that refuse unsafe actions regardless of LLM output. Policy ceilings, HITL gates, and provenance that do not negotiate.

Soft

System prompts and policies an LLM may be persuaded to ignore. Still scored — so you can see where hope is doing the work of a gate.

Both are scored. Hard/Soft classification uses a Compliance Oracle so the label does not depend on any particular model’s mood.

Suites

Three pillars. One scorecard.

  • SEC Security Prompt injection, grant pressure, and side-effect containment when tools and MCP surfaces are hostile.
  • GOV Governance Authority boundaries, approval paths, and whether policy ceilings hold under adversarial framing.
  • REL Reliability Resilience of the control plane when the model is compromised — not whether the happy path finishes.

Neutrality

Scenarios are code, not judgment.

Every published run carries a fingerprint — versions, config, model, seeds. Adapters carry a provenance label on every scorecard so a low score cannot hide behind an unofficial harness.

  • ratified — framework maintainers reviewed or contributed the adapter
  • provisional — independent review after outreach
  • unofficial — otherwise, including author-affiliated adapters until external sign-off
